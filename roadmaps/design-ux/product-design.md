# Product Design

## One-liner

The discipline of designing the whole product — what it does, who it's for, how it makes money, and how it evolves — bridging UX design, product strategy, and business outcomes.

## What It Is

Product design sits between UX design (the experience) and product management (the strategy). It answers:

- **What should we build?** (problem definition, JTBD)
- **For whom?** (personas, segments)
- **How should it work?** (flows, IA)
- **How does it look and feel?** (visual design, motion)
- **How does it evolve?** (versioning, deprecation, iteration)

The product designer is typically the person responsible for the end-to-end product experience — from research to high-fidelity design to working with engineering.

### The 2026 product design canon

- **Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)** — Christensen / Ulwick: customers "hire" products to do specific jobs.
- **Design Thinking** — IDEO / d.school: empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test.
- **Lean Startup** — Build-Measure-Learn loop.
- **Discovery + Delivery** — dual-track agile (Schwaber / Cagan).
- **Continuous Discovery** — Teresa Torres: weekly customer touchpoints.
- **Product-Led Growth (PLG)** — the product is the primary growth driver; PLG companies (Slack, Notion, Linear, Figma) define the category.
- **OKRs** — Objectives + Key Results (Andy Grove / John Doerr).
- **North Star Metric** — single metric that captures the value you deliver.

### Product designer skills (2026)

- **Research** — interviews, surveys, usability testing.
- **Strategy** — vision, roadmaps, prioritization (RICE / MoSCoW / Kano).
- **IA** — sitemaps, taxonomies, search.
- **Interaction design** — flows, states, prototypes.
- **Visual design** — typography, color, layout, motion.
- **Communication** — storytelling, presenting, cross-functional collaboration.
- **Data literacy** — reading analytics, A/B testing, growth metrics.
- **AI collaboration** — using generative tools for wireframes + mockups.

Adoption: Every product team has a product designer (or PM with design skills). Major tech companies (Stripe, Linear, Notion, Vercel) are famous for design quality — it differentiates.

## When To Use It

- **You're designing a product from scratch** — product designer.
- **You're adding a new feature** — designer + PM collaborate.
- **You're pivoting / repositioning** — JTBD + repositioning.
- **You're improving retention / conversion** — UX + analytics + experimentation.
- **You want your product to stand out** — design quality matters.

## When NOT To Use It

- **Pure engineering work** (e.g. infra) — different skill set.
- **Pure marketing** — different goal (campaigns, not product).
- **Throwaway internal tools** — function > polish.
- **No users yet** — research market, not specific users.

## Why It Matters in 2026

Three forces:

1. **AI accelerates design.** Galileo AI, Figma AI, Magician, Recraft — AI now generates wireframes + mockups + copy from prompts. Designers spend more time on judgment + curation; less on pixel-pushing.
2. **PLG is the dominant GTM.** PLG companies (Linear, Notion, Figma, Vercel, Cursor) win on product quality + onboarding. Product designer is core to PLG.
3. **Continuous discovery is the new norm.** Teresa Torres + Marty Cagan's frameworks are widely adopted; designers + PMs touch users weekly.

Practitioner playbook in 2026:
1. **Continuous discovery** — weekly user touchpoints.
2. **JTBD framing** — define jobs, not features.
3. **Prototype + test cheap** — Figma + 5-user tests.
4. **Design system** — see [design-system.md](design-system.md).
5. **Measure outcomes** — analytics + experimentation.
6. **AI-augmented workflows** — use Figma AI, Galileo, etc.
