# User Research

## One-liner

The practice of understanding your users through interviews, observation, surveys, analytics, and usability tests — so every product decision is grounded in evidence, not opinion.

## What It Is

User research is the systematic study of users — their goals, needs, behaviors, pain points — to inform product decisions. It includes:

| Method | Type | When |
|--------|------|------|
| **User interviews** | Qualitative (1:1) | Deep understanding of motivations; persona creation. |
| **Usability testing** | Qualitative (observed) | Find UX issues before launch. |
| **Surveys** | Quantitative (many) | Validate at scale; measure satisfaction. |
| **Card sorting** | Qualitative | Information architecture. |
| **A/B testing** | Quantitative | Compare two designs. |
| **Field studies / Contextual inquiry** | Qualitative | Observe users in their environment. |
| **Diary studies** | Longitudinal | Track behavior over days/weeks. |
| **Tree testing** | Quantitative | Validate IA with realistic tasks. |
| **First-click testing** | Quantitative | Find IA issues early. |
| **Analytics analysis** | Quantitative | What users actually do. |
| **Heatmaps / Session replay** | Quantitative | Visualize behavior. |

### Attitudinal vs Behavioral

| Type | Asks | Examples |
|------|------|----------|
| **Attitudinal** | What users *say* | Interviews, surveys. |
| **Behavioral** | What users *do* | Analytics, usability testing, A/B tests. |

**Best practice**: combine both. People say one thing, do another.

### The research process

1. **Define objectives** — what decision will this inform?
2. **Choose method** — interviews / usability test / survey / analytics.
3. **Recruit participants** — representative of target users.
4. **Conduct research** — with rigor (no leading questions, etc.).
5. **Synthesize** — affinity mapping, themes, personas.
6. **Share insights** — research reports, storyboards, presentations.
7. **Apply** — influence product decisions.

### Continuous Discovery (Teresa Torres)

The modern best practice:
- **Weekly customer touchpoints** (interviews, support tickets, analytics).
- **Opportunity Solution Tree** — visualize the path from outcomes → opportunities → solutions → tests.
- **Always-on research** — not just project-based.

Adoption: User research is the foundation of every design-led company. NN/g certifications; thousands of UX research teams; CI/CD for research via repositories like Dovetail.

## When To Use It

- **You're starting a new product** — discovery interviews.
- **You're validating a design** — usability testing.
- **You want to prioritize features** — surveys + interviews.
- **You want to measure satisfaction** — NPS, CSAT surveys.
- **You're stuck on a design decision** — A/B test.
- **You want to find your personas** — interviews + analytics.

## When NOT To Use It

- **You have zero users** — market research is different.
- **You're optimizing a known UX issue** — analytics + A/B test directly.
- **You have no time** — at minimum, do 5 usability tests.
- **You don't want to hear bad news** — then don't do research.

## Why It Matters in 2026

Three forces:

1. **Continuous Discovery is the norm.** Teresa Torres' framework widely adopted; weekly user touchpoints are table stakes for product teams.
2. **AI-augmented research.** Synthetic users (controversial), AI-summarized interviews (Dovetail AI, Notably AI), automated tagging.
3. **Research repositories.** Dovetail, Notably, Notion — research as a first-class artifact, not slides that disappear.

Practitioner playbook in 2026:
1. **Weekly user touchpoint** — even 30 minutes counts.
2. **Opportunity Solution Tree** — visualize decisions.
3. **Mix attitudinal + behavioral** — what they say + what they do.
4. **Research repository** — Dovetail or Notion; don't lose insights.
5. **Recruit representative users** — not friends + family.

## Comparison With Alternatives

| Alternative | Better when | Worse when |
|-------------|-------------|------------|
| **Interviews** | Deep, exploratory. | You need scale. |
| **Surveys** | Scale; validation. | "Why" questions. |
| **Usability testing** | Finding UX issues. | Measuring behavior. |
| **Analytics** | What users do. | Why they do it. |
| **A/B testing** | Two-designs comparison. | Exploratory. |
| **No research** | Never (for real products). | — |
