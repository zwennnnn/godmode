# Game Design

## One-liner

The discipline of designing what a game is — mechanics, dynamics, aesthetics, narrative, level design, balance, and the player experience — independent of any specific engine.

## What It Is

Game design is the art + science of defining what a game does and how it feels. It includes:

| Discipline | What |
|------------|------|
| **Mechanics** | Rules, verbs, systems. |
| **Dynamics** | How mechanics play out over time. |
| **Aesthetics** | The emotional responses evoked (Hunicke et al.'s MDA framework). |
| **Narrative** | Story, world-building, characters. |
| **Level design** | Spaces, pacing, encounters. |
| **Systems design** | Economy, progression, balance. |
| **Player experience** | Onboarding, flow, motivation loops. |
| **Game feel** | Juice, responsiveness, audio-visual feedback. |

### Key frameworks

| Framework | Source | Idea |
|-----------|--------|------|
| **MDA** (Mechanics, Dynamics, Aesthetics) | Hunicke, LeBlanc, Zubek | Designers craft mechanics; players experience aesthetics. |
| **Bartle's Player Types** | Richard Bartle | Achievers, Explorers, Socializers, Killers. |
| **Bartle's Player Motivations** | Later revision | Achievement, Exploration, Socializing, Immersion, Griefing. |
| **Self-Determination Theory** | Ryan & Deci | Competence, Autonomy, Relatedness. |
| **Octalysis** | Yu-kai Chou | 8 core drives of gamification. |
| **Flow** | Csikszentmihalyi | Challenge = Skill; the sweet spot. |
| **Schell's Lens** | Jesse Schell | 100 lenses for game design. |
| **The 4 Fun Keys** | Nicole Lazzaro | Hard, Easy, Serious, People fun. |

### The design process

1. **Concept** — what's the elevator pitch? (one sentence)
2. **Pitch** — formal GDD (Game Design Document).
3. **Prototype** — paper prototype, graybox, or digital.
4. **Iteration** — playtest → learn → iterate.
5. **Ship** — minimal viable game.

### Design artifacts

| Artifact | What |
|---------|------|
| **Game Design Document (GDD)** | Master document; vision, mechanics, systems, narrative. |

Adoption: Game design is the foundation. Every shipped game has a designer. Indie devs often wear many hats; AAA teams have dedicated designers.

## When To Use It

- **You're designing any game** — period.
- **You want to evaluate a game** — design frameworks help.
- **You're improving an existing game** — iterate via design.
- **You're teaching / explaining games** — frameworks clarify.
- **You're applying game design to non-games** — gamification (Octalysis, SDT).

## When NOT To Use It

- **You just want to code** — design comes before code, but you can prototype without it.
- **You're a tools / engine developer** — different discipline.
- **You're a pure artist** — design intersects but isn't your job.

## Why It Matters in 2026

Three forces:

1. **AI-assisted design.** GPTs generate GDDs, balance spreadsheets, level layouts. Designers curate + refine.
2. **Live ops + content updates demand ongoing design.** GaaS games ship quarterly content; design never stops.
3. **Player research matured.** Continuous playtesting + analytics inform every decision.

Practitioner playbook in 2026:
1. **Start with the experience** — what do you want the player to *feel*?
2. **Mechanics > aesthetics** — build the system, then the feel.
3. **Prototype paper first** — before code.
4. **Playtest early + often** — 5 people is enough.
5. **Iterate ruthlessly** — cut what doesn't serve the experience.
